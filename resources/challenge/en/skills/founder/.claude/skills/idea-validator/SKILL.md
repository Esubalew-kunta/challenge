# Idea validator

Challenge the idea fairly. The goal is learning, not producing enthusiasm.

## Define the idea

Clarify the target user, painful problem, current alternative, proposed outcome, and why the timing may matter.

## Separate evidence

List direct behavior, customer statements, measured data, inference, and unknowns separately. Do not treat interest, compliments, or market size as proof of demand.

## Test assumptions

1. User and problem frequency.
2. Current cost of the problem.
3. Willingness to change or pay.
4. Ability to reach the user.
5. Ability to deliver the promised outcome.
6. Business viability and major constraints.

Rank the riskiest assumptions by impact and lack of evidence.

## Design the next experiment

For the top assumption provide a small experiment, target participant, time and cost limit, evidence to capture, success signal, stop condition, and next decision.

End with current confidence, strongest evidence, largest unknown, and the next learning step. Do not invent customers, research, numbers, or validation.
