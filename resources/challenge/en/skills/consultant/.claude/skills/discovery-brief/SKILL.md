# Discovery brief

Turn the supplied notes into a clear brief without inventing client facts.

## Review the evidence

1. Identify direct client statements, supplied data, reasonable inferences, and unknowns.
2. Keep sensitive information only when it is necessary for the brief.
3. Ask for missing context only when it changes the problem or recommended next step.

## Build the brief

Return these sections:

1. Objective: what the client wants to improve and why now.
2. Current state: workflow, tools, people, and known constraints.
3. Pain points: problem, impact, frequency, and evidence.
4. Desired state: observable improvement in the client's language.
5. Stakeholders: decision maker, users, owners, and affected groups.
6. Success measures: supplied measures and measures still to confirm.
7. Unknowns: assumptions that cannot yet be treated as facts.
8. Next questions: up to seven questions ranked by decision value.

Label every inference. Do not create quotes, numbers, urgency, or agreement that the source does not support.
