### 4.2 — "What we refuse to deploy" (4 cards)
- **Section header:** badge `Our convictions` · H2 `What we refuse to deploy` · sub `Compliance is a floor. Our convictions come first. Four limits we apply to every deployment, whatever the regulatory calendar.`
- **Proposed (EN):**
  1. **No automated decisions about people** — `Recruitment, evaluation, sanctions: AI prepares the analysis, a human decides. Always. A position consistent with the EU AI Act, which classes these uses as high-risk.`
  2. **No systems your teams don't understand** — `Every system we deliver is documented, explainable and reversible. If we can't explain to you how it decides, we don't ship it.`
  3. **No client data in model training** — `Your data serves your systems, not the providers' training. It's configured, written down and verifiable.`
  4. **No deployment without a human in the loop** — `Content going out to your clients, a figure going to the board: critical outputs get human validation until reliability is proven on your use cases, measured on your data. That's the principle we then write into your AI charter.`
  - Closing line: `These limits are what make the transformation durable — documentation and reversibility survive staff turnover and audits.`
- **Rationale:** Faithful. The high-risk classification of HR uses is an accurate AI Act characterisation.

### 4.3 — "The deadlines that concern you" (EU AI Act timeline)
- **Section header:** badge `The calendar` · H2 `The deadlines that concern you` · sub `The EU AI Act has been in force since August 2024, with a phased application. Here are the milestones that matter for a company using AI, without the jargon.`
- **Proposed (EN):**

  | Date | Title | Detail |
  |---|---|---|
  | February 2025 | AI literacy (Article 4) | `The EU AI Act requires companies that use AI to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among their teams. Measures proportionate to your context, with no mandatory certification.` |
  | August 2026 | Chatbot transparency (Article 50) | `From 2 August 2026, your users must know they're interacting with an AI, and generated content must be labelled where applicable. Authorities can also start checking AI literacy from this date.` |
  | December 2027 `[to validate]` | High-risk obligations | `Obligations for high-risk systems — notably HR uses such as recruitment and employee evaluation — were pushed to December 2027 by the June 2026 omnibus. Room to prepare calmly.` |
  | Ongoing | GDPR and CNIL guidance | `Legal basis, data minimisation, impact assessment (DPIA) for high-risk processing. France's CNIL publishes practical AI sheets and recommends framing generative AI with an internal charter.` |

- **`[to validate]` flag:** The **December 2027 high-risk deferral via a "June 2026 omnibus"** is after my knowledge cutoff (Jan 2026) and is a specific, movable legal date — flagged for legal review before publish. Under the AI Act text as originally adopted, high-risk obligations (Annex III) apply from **2 August 2026** and product-embedded high-risk (Annex I) from **2 August 2027**; the June 2026 "Digital Omnibus" deferral must be confirmed against the final adopted amendment. **February 2025 (Art. 4 AI literacy + prohibited practices), 2 August 2026 (Art. 50 transparency), and "in force since August 2024" are correct as stated.**
- **Rationale:** Translate faithfully but do not present a post-cutoff legal date as settled fact.

### 4.4 — "What we put in place at your company" (6 deliverables)
- **Section header:** badge `Our approach` · H2 `What we put in place at your company` · sub `Compliance isn't a separate project. It's built into the audit and into every system we deliver.`
- **Proposed (EN):**
  1. **System mapping and classification** — `During the audit, every existing or planned AI system is logged and classified against the AI Act's risk categories. You know exactly where you stand.`
  2. **An AI charter built together** — `Permitted and prohibited uses, data never to be entered, human validation of outputs, a point of contact: the content the CNIL recommends, adapted to your teams, from the audit phase.`
  3. **Transparency by default in agents** — `Every chatbot and agent we deliver clearly states it's an AI. The Article 50 requirement is built in by design, from the first line of the agent.`
  4. **A GDPR layer on every deployment** — `Legal basis, data minimisation and informing data subjects are handled for each system, in coordination with your DPO where you have one.`
  5. **A register of systems and training** — `An internal register documents your AI systems and the training carried out. That's what lets you demonstrate your approach in the event of a check.`
  6. **Built-in regulatory watch** — `The framework moves; we track it for you. A concrete example: the June 2026 omnibus and the deferral of high-risk obligations were folded into our engagements as soon as adopted.` `[to validate — June 2026 omnibus, see 4.3]`

### 4.5 — "The real level of risk, without the drama"
- **Section header:** badge `Without the drama` · H2 `The real level of risk, without the drama`
- **Proposed (EN):**
  - `Most business use cases fall under minimal risk in the sense of the EU AI Act: reporting, lead qualification, internal copilots, summaries. The heavy obligations concern specific categories, such as HR uses of recruitment or evaluation, from December 2027 `[to validate]`. So the point isn't to rush everything into compliance in a panic: it's to know which of your systems are concerned, and which aren't. That's exactly the role of the mapping done during the audit.`
  - `For scale: the EU AI Act provides for fines of up to €15 million or 3% of turnover for transparency breaches (for SMEs, the lower amount applies), and the GDPR up to €20 million or 4%. All the more reason to handle this calmly, upfront.`
- **Rationale:** Penalty figures match AI Act Art. 99 (€15M/3% for obligations other than prohibited practices) and GDPR Art. 83 (€20M/4%) — accurate. December 2027 carries the same [to validate].
